§ 1

Purpose, scope and starting points

The practice asks one question of any system: can it still discover that it is wrong, and act on that, as its power grows? It applies to systems that combine people, institutions, technologies and habitats. It is a set of questions and proposals, not a discipline or a decision engine.

Scope, declared before testing. A practice that explains away every null result cannot be tested, so the expected results come first.

A null result in the first row is a passed prediction. A null result in the second or third row counts against the practice.

Starting points. Four are descriptive claims:

¶ P1. Accounts of complex systems are situated and incomplete.

¶ P2. Updating inside a model does not show that the model is adequate.

¶ P3. Representations can change what they represent.

¶ P4. Lives depend on material, social and ecological conditions, some of them hard to rebuild.

Four are adopted commitments, not findings:

¶ P5. Moral consideration follows welfare, with precaution where sentience is credibly uncertain.

¶ P6. Care is not earned by contribution. Arbitrary power and permanent outgroups are opposed.

¶ P7. Flourishing is plural; no single quantity is maximized.

¶ P8. Greater consequential power should not erode the capacity to discover and act on inadequacy.

One is a procedural wager:

¶ P9. Use accountable review, staged change and tested successors rather than an unrevisable final judge. This manages fallibility; it does not prove the procedure right.
