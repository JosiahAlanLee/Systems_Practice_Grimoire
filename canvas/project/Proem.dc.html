Revisable Systems Practice — Revision 5

A practice for studying and governing systems that mix people, institutions, machines and habitats, while keeping its own models, goals and rules open to challenge. Its commitments are care, non-domination and the conditions of flourishing. Revision 5 restores five distinctive claims as propositions that can fail, and shows the practice applied, including where it added nothing.

What changed from Revision 4

Revision 5 keeps Revision 4's narrowed commitments and puts back what made the practice distinct, as claims that can fail.

¶ Five distinctive claims return (§4): decay ownership, temporal legibility, identity–lesson separation, directional corrigibility with a proposed measure, and displacement as a coded variable. Each states what would count against it.

¶ Scope is declared before testing (§1): where the practice should add nothing, and where it should add most.

¶ Three worked examples replace most citations (§§8–10): a hypothetical outage, a real test run with a null result, and an analogy followed until it stopped being useful.

¶ "Test the successor first" gains an extremis clause (§2): where shocks outrun a staged switch, the successor must exist before it is needed.

¶ Citations move out of the text. A short list of debts gives the map, and full references close the document.
