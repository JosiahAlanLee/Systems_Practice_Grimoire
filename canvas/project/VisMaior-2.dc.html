Of its Names

It is called Force Majeure by the French, Höhere Gewalt by the Germans, Fuerza Mayor and Forza Maggiore in the south, the Act of God by the English, and by the old jurists Vis Maior, the greater force. It answers to all of these. It signs none of them.

Of its Office

It excuseth performance. It suspendeth the hour. Under it no penalty accrueth and no credit is owed. It hath dominion over flood, fire, storm and the shaking of the ground; over war, riot and the closing of roads; over the strike, the embargo and the order of the magistrate; over the failure of carriers, the failure of the mains, and the cutting of cable by persons unknown.

Of its Summoning

It is not summoned. It is written in beforehand, in the smallest type, signed by both parties and filed. Writing, signing, forgetting: that is the whole of the operation, and it is performed in every office before every bargain. Thereafter it cometh of itself, when the loss is no one's fault.

Of its Notice

It hath no power until it is named. Name it in writing, promptly, to the other party. Set down what it preventeth and for how long. Show that thou didst strive against it. Then it standeth between thee and every claim.

Of its Departure

It departeth when the circumstance endeth. If it tarry past the term the parties wrote, either may end the bargain, and that ending is its only banishing.

Of its Critics

It hath none. No party speaketh against it, for each would have it. In the hour of loss the storm is cursed and the clause is kept, and the next bargain copieth it word for word.
