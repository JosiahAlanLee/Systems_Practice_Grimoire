§ 5 · Power, care and representation

§ 5

Power, care and representation

Non-domination concerns exposure to uncontrolled interference, even when power is exercised kindly. Material dependence can enable domination but does not prove it. Ask who controls interference, whose interests count and what contestation is actually available.

Enforcement. Disapproval does not correct an actor that others depend on. Specify who must respond, what they can be required to do, how refusal is handled and who reviews the reviewer. Consequences can remove obstructive authority without using destitution as the sanction.

Visibility read as command. Where people coordinate without designated leaders, outsiders measure visibility and read it as authority. The press, funders and investigators then appoint the centre the network refused to have, and the hazard falls on the most visible people. Jo Freeman described this in 1970; it remains a design problem for any leaderless effort, and a reason D1 matters.

Care. Care and non-domination are adopted values. Dependency does not logically create the obligation; the commitment does. The traditions the practice draws on disagree with each other, and their disagreements stay relevant.
