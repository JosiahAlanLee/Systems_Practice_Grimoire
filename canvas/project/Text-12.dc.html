§ 12

Open questions, left open

None of these is resolved here, and nothing in the practice requires them to be.

¶ No accountable recipient. Where the corrective authority is itself the hazard, the audits diagnose but the remedies have nowhere to go. Federalism, courts and a free press are routes around any one centre; where none exists, the practice has nothing to maintain.

¶ Representatives in networks built without them. The patch for interests without votes is trustees, and leaderless coordination refuses designated roles. How such a network gives standing to people who cannot afford to be visible, without creating the centre it avoids, is unanswered.

¶ The refuge dependency. Legible coordination may depend on the institutions this practice maintains. One acts inside the refuge; the other keeps it standing.

¶ Records versus fading. Fading protects people; records let systems learn. Identity–lesson separation (D3) is a partial answer, not a resolution.

¶ Single-owner infrastructure. When a critical function already has one owner, the practice does not say whether public ownership, a public option alongside private ones, or polycentric replacement is better. The bypass test applies to a public owner as much as a private one.

¶ Who judges preservation. Someone decides whether a successor kept the old rule's protective functions. The regress is managed by exposing it, not ended.

¶ Enforcement, allocation and cross-scale coordination, as in Revision 4: how to enforce without arbitrary power, how to allocate under incompatible claims, and how decisions at one scale or timescale bind another.
