D4 Directional corrigibility, measured

Claim. A warning sign: consequential power rising while the capacity to discover and act on inadequacy falls.

Measure (proposed). Track two series over time. Power: deployment scope, market share, or the number of people who depend on the system. Correction: the time from a substantiated report to action by someone authorized, the share of substantiated reports that reach such a person, and the number of independent routes to contest a decision.

Counts against it. Diverging series predict nothing about later serious failures, compared with systems whose correction capacity keeps pace with their power.

D5 Displacement, coded

Claim. After disruption, systems that preserve the capacity to reorganize recover better than systems that restore a prior configuration, when hazards change faster than the configuration can.

Measure. Code each recovery as a displacement vector across mission, structure, membership, capabilities and accountability, each scored from unchanged to replaced. Regulation (return to set point) and revision (return displaced) are the ends of each axis, not a binary label.

Counts against it. Displacement predicts nothing about later function, or the coding fails to separate cases because nearly every human recovery involves some change.
