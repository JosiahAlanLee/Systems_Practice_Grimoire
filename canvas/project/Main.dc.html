Inference: locate the break, verify alarms, arrange access, estimate repair time.

Criticism: rebuild the timeline. When was the diagnosis available, which authorization was missing, and what evidence supports the claim of obstruction?

Constitution: why does rank rather than technical competence control dispatch, and who can change that rule?

Audits. Map customers, field crews, carrier partners, technicians and executives, keeping responsibility, expertise and control separate. A technician who cannot safely challenge a manager is part of the model, not a personality defect. “Fast escalation” as a measure can reward notifying executives while repairs wait.

Measure. Split diagnosis-to-dispatch from dispatch-to-restoration. The first is organizational delay; the second is mostly physical repair. This split is the most directly testable idea in the practice, and regulator investigations of outages often carry timelines detailed enough to apply it.

Proposal. Trial standing technical dispatch authority under specified conditions, keeping executive notification, a shared record and independent review. Compare restoration delays and mistaken dispatches by severity. Name who must act on a substantiated obstruction, and with what appeal; popularity, shame and bonuses do not substitute for authority over people and resources.
