§ 4

Five distinctive claims

These are the practice's own proposals. Each borrows from an established literature, adds something that literature does not state, and can fail independently of the others. D3 and D5 rest on single examples so far, and D4's measure is untested.

D1 Decay ownership

Claim. Whoever controls how long a signal persists controls whether a coordinating network can be mapped after the fact. Coordination through public signs is only as safe as participants' control over decay, or the control of a third party bound to published rules.

Beyond its antecedents. Research on stigmergy treats evaporation as a property of the medium. On digital platforms, evaporation is a retention policy set by an owner, and signals that fade for participants can persist for someone else.

Counts against it. Networks whose signals are retained by third parties prove no easier to reconstruct or disrupt than comparable networks whose signals actually expire.

D2 Temporal legibility

Claim. A public schedule lowers coordination costs for members and raises exposure to anyone else who can read it. Predictable timing gets gamed by the measured and exploited by adversaries.

Evidence so far. US nursing homes slack after an inspection and ramp up before the next. In 2003 about a third of their inspections were predictable in timing, partly because of the maximum-interval rule itself. Ransomware groups have timed major attacks to holiday weekends.

Beyond its antecedents. Focal points and Goodhart's law are usually treated separately. D2 treats a clock as both a coordination resource and an attack surface, and asks how much of a network's timing should be public.

Counts against it. Moving to unpredictable timing yields no gain in measured performance or in exposure, after controlling for frequency.

D3 Identity–lesson separation

Claim. Where being identifiable is penalized, systems that keep learning separate who reported from what was learned. Identity decays on a rule; the lesson persists. Systems that cannot separate the two lose reports or lose reporters.

Example. NASA's aviation safety reporting system returns the identifying strip to the reporter, keeps no copy and stores de-identified reports. In its first quarter, over 99% of reporters identified themselves, though a penalty waiver also rewarded doing so.

The witness who is punished teaches the instrument to report calm.
