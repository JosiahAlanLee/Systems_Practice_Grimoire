§ 11

Testing program

Each claim gets a setting where it could fail, a measure, and a result that would count against it.

Claim

Where to test

Measure

Counts against it

Bypass audit and D1

Dependents of single-owner failures

Who had a working route that avoided the owner; restoration time by route

Dependents with and without routes recover alike

D2

Inspection regimes; incident timing

Performance near scheduled dates against other dates; incidents by staffing period

No difference after controlling for frequency

D3

Reporting systems with and without protection

Report volume, reuse of lessons, repeat failures

Separation shows no learning gain once incentives are controlled

D4

Agentic platforms and their providers over time

Power series against report-to-action time, share of reports reaching authority, routes to contest

Divergence predicts nothing about later serious failures

D5

Historical closures and revivals of organizations

Displacement vector against later function

No predictive value, or coding fails to separate cases

The practice as a whole

Three-arm review of unseen cases

Specific, supported, actionable findings, scored by blind judges

The practice adds nothing over an equal-length checklist, or adds more unsupported claims

Rules for every test.

¶ Define recovery before looking: restored service, restored capability, retained autonomy, or capacity for further revision. They are not interchangeable.

¶ Unknown is not absent. A report's silence about retaliation is not evidence that none occurred.

¶ Use historical, aggregate or consented material for political cases. Never produce person-level or site-level maps of living networks.

¶ Tag provenance: inspected evidence, secondary synthesis, or model-generated hypothesis.

¶ Set the smallest useful gain and the acceptable error burden before running a confirmatory comparison.

Do not repair the witness to fit the instrument.
