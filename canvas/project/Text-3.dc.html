§ 3

Four audits

Each audit is a question to put to a system. None yields a verdict by itself.

Speed of correction. How long does a problem take to reach someone able to act, and how long from there to action? Elections correct on a cycle of years; deployments change in weeks. Protected, non-punitive reporting adds a continuous channel between those bursts, but a channel needs a durable store or its signals fade before anyone acts.

Captured options: the bypass test. For identity, discovery, validation, coordination and appeal, find the controlling centres. Ask whether a person can secure correction without their discretionary cooperation. The alternative must resist the specific capture or failure being examined; another interface over the same owner is not a route.

Two further questions belong here:

¶ Does the centre expire? Authority that ends on schedule is a route around it through time. Seasonal offices, term limits and sunset clauses are examples.

¶ Who controls decay? Whoever sets how long signals persist controls what can be reconstructed later (D1).

Interests without votes. Who is affected without being involved? Animals, children, future people, dormant members and people who cannot safely be visible are the usual cases. For those unable to represent themselves, use plural, contestable, term-limited trustees, giving way to direct participation where possible. Choosing trustees already selects interests.

Gamed measures. Mark measures whose use changes what they measure, and do not treat a gamed number's success as proof. Look for displaced costs, reclassification and strategic timing.

Before any audit: who receives the report? The remedies assume an accountable recipient. Where the corrective authority is itself the hazard, the audits still diagnose, but the practice has no one to report to (§12).
